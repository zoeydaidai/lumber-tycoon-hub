Evergreen route guide

Lumber Tycoon 2 rare woods guide

The useful question is not “which wood is best?” It is which route fits your vehicle, toolset, storage space, and the job you are doing right now.

Updated September 28, 2026Route planningCheck in-game availability

Start with a purpose, not a rarity label

Use nearby trees when you need a fast build batch or early money. Make longer trips only when the material, color, or collection goal makes the extra hauling worthwhile. Wood availability, event spawns, and route conditions can change, so treat community lists as route references—not permanent price promises.

Useful region-to-wood starting points

Main Biome: Oak, Cherry, and Elm

Best for a first run because it is close to the spawn area, shops, and your loading routine. Cherry Meadow is the natural follow-up when you specifically want Cherry wood without turning a short session into an expedition.

Safari and Mountainside: Walnut and Birch

These are sensible mid-range pickups when you are already moving north or up the map. Bring a vehicle with room to turn before you cut; a slightly smaller clean load beats a huge log you cannot unload.

Taiga: Fir, Pine, and higher-commitment routes

Taiga is a stock-up trip, not an emergency cash run. Know how you will return, leave room for the trip back, and do not assume a seasonal tree will be present outside its event window.

Tropics and caves: Koa, Cavecrawler, and specialty runs

These routes reward preparation. If you are heading into dark or tight terrain, take a Work Light and use a vehicle that fits the route. Do not turn a rare-wood run into a blind maze search.

Volcano and later-game materials

Volcano/Lava wood and other remote materials make more sense once you can haul safely. Read the volcano and cave prep guide before leaving base, especially if your session time is short.

A low-risk wood run loop

  1. Choose one target: sellable planks, a blueprint material, or one storage category.
  2. Take only the axe, light, and vehicle setup that route needs.
  3. Cut a load you can actually move and mill without blocking your base.
  4. Process or store it before starting another long trip.
